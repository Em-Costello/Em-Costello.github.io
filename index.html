Research

My research focuses on how supermassive black holes shape the haloes they live in, specifically in the group and cluster regime. Energy released by accreting black holes heats and expels gas from these galaxy groups and clusters, changing their thermodynamic properties and redistributing matter on the scales that large-scale structure surveys use to measure cosmology.

My work uses the FLAMINGO suite of large-volume cosmological hydrodynamical simulations to trace the co-evolution of hot gas and black holes across cosmic time, and to understand how baryonic feedback and cosmology together set the observable properties of groups and clusters. I also use data from the Planck satellite and the Atacama Cosmology Telescope to probe the effects of feedback in massive haloes observationally via the thermal Sunyaev–Zel'dovich effect. I'm supervised by Prof Ian McCarthy at the Astrophysics Research Institute in Liverpool, and funded through the Liv.INNO Centre for Doctoral Training in data-intensive science.

Alongside my PhD I also work as a data scientist on a DiRAC-supported placement with the Guy's and St Thomas' NHS Foundation Trust, King's College London, and Evelina Children's Hospital, studying how data and research move across the clinical–academic boundary. Specifically, I've been working with Prof Ingrid Wolfe and the Children and Young People's Health Partnership (CYPHP) on childhood asthma, one of the most common long-term conditions affecting children. Using data including prescriptions, hospital attendances and indicators of asthma control, my research aims to estimate how many children may be at risk of poorly controlled asthma, understand patterns in their healthcare use and identify where unmet need might be greatest. This research fits into the longer-term ambition to help make care more proactive: identifying children who may need additional support before their condition deteriorates, rather than waiting until they require emergency treatment.
