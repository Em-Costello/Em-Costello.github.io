Research

I study how supermassive black holes shape the haloes they live in. Energy released by accreting black holes heats and expels gas from galaxy groups and clusters, changing their X-ray and Sunyaev–Zel'dovich properties and redistributing matter on the scales that large-scale structure surveys use to measure cosmology.

My work uses the FLAMINGO suite of large-volume cosmological hydrodynamical simulations to trace the co-evolution of hot gas and black holes across cosmic time, and to understand how baryonic feedback and cosmology together set the observable properties of groups and clusters. I'm supervised by Prof Ian McCarthy, Dr Jaime Salcido and Prof Robert Crain, and funded through the Liv.INNO Centre for Doctoral Training in data-intensive science.

Alongside the PhD I work as a data scientist on a DiRAC-supported project with the KCL Department of Women and Young People's Health and Evelina Children's Hospital, studying how data and research move across the clinical–academic boundary.
